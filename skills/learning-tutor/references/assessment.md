# Assessment and remediation

## Before presenting a check

Set the outcome, question/task coverage, permitted aids, feedback timing, and rubric or threshold. `pass_threshold: 0.8` is a configurable starting point for scored checks, not a universal mastery standard. Essential prerequisite or artifact criteria can remain mandatory even when the aggregate score passes. Agree on those criteria before the attempt.

Practice checks can give immediate feedback. Summative checks should avoid teaching an answer that a later item depends on. One item at a time is the default for chat; allow batches when requested. Keep solutions separate from questions until the learner attempts them or asks to see them.

Solve every item independently. Verify assumptions, units, domain, wording, and whether the response format permits the intended answer. If ambiguous or defective, explain the defect, exclude it from grading, retain the original record, and issue a corrected replacement. Do not count a tutor's faulty question as a learner error.

## Multiple-choice questions

Write the correct content and meaningful distractors first. A distractor should reflect a plausible error, not arbitrary noise; a selection is evidence of a possible cause, not proof of that cause. Verify that exactly one answer is defensible unless explicitly using multiple selection.

Randomly shuffle options with an available RNG when practical. Carry the correct answer identity through the shuffle and recompute the displayed answer key. Re-solve and verify the final key against the displayed content. Without an RNG, vary placement and check for obvious patterns, but do not claim true randomization. Do not force alternation or promise a balanced distribution: those constraints can themselves become clues. Natural repetition in a short random sequence is not a reason to invalidate an attempt.

Freeze wording and options once issued. Grade the version the learner saw, never a silently edited one. Store the final correct answer and reasoning with the response so a stale index cannot quietly change the grade. Do not reveal an active answer key in the chat; local files are not a secure exam system, so this is practice rather than proctored assessment.

## Other evidence

Use explanation and derivation rubrics that identify the required reasoning and acceptable alternatives. Do not penalize irrelevant formatting or language mistakes unless those are assessed outcomes.

For artifacts, use the task's actual environment and requirements. Run permitted checks when available; keep unexecuted checks labeled unverified. Writing and qualitative reasoning need a rubric and evidence excerpts, not invented numerical precision. Avoid equating a runnable artifact with understanding; add explanation or transfer when the stated goal requires it.

## Feedback and logging

Append a concise record to `.learning/attempts.md`: date, stage/task identifier, source or generated variant, actual response or artifact path, aids/hints, result, correct reasoning or rubric evidence, identified weakness, and next action. For MCQ, include the issued options or an immutable question snapshot and the final answer key. Record enough to audit a grade without copying large copyrighted documents.

Corrections are new entries referencing the original attempt. Preserve the original response and append-only history. If the denominator changes after invalidating a question, record the revised score explicitly.

Remediate the weak skills, use fresh variants, and retain previously demonstrated skills. A narrow successful retest establishes the repaired skill; it does not erase an unrelated untested criterion. After roughly three unsuccessful attempts, suggest a prerequisite lesson, smaller unit, or pause. Respect the learner's choice and avoid unbounded retesting.
